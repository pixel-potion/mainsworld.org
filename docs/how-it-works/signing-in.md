# Signing in

Main's World has no password, no username to remember, and no email to confirm.
Your phone is your key.

## On your phone

Open [mains.world](https://mains.world) inside [World Money](https://world.org/world-money)
and approve the sign-in. World Money is the wallet app formerly called World App.
That proves you control the World Money wallet you are using; it does not by itself
record a human-verification check for your Main. That's the whole sign-in.

If a generic World link opens the separate World ID app or says Main's World is
unlisted, open the **Apps** tab in World Money and search for **Main's World**.
World ID is the separate app for identity proofs.

If you're new, see [the worlds & privacy](/how-it-works/the-worlds) for how
privacy works, and [the economy](/the-economy) for how verification is shown.

## On a computer

You can also use Main's World in an ordinary web browser on a laptop or desktop
computer. Because signing in happens through World Money, and World Money lives on
your phone, the computer asks your phone to vouch for it.

Here's how it goes:

1. Open [mains.world](https://mains.world) in your browser and choose **LOG IN**.
   A card appears with a **square code** (a QR code) and a short **four-character
   code** underneath it.
2. Scan the square with World Money on your phone.
3. Your phone opens a page asking whether you want to sign a computer in. It
   names the browser it's being asked about and shows the same four characters
   the computer is showing.
4. Check that the four characters match, then approve. The computer signs itself
   in a moment later.

If you decide it wasn't you, choose the "wasn't me" option instead and the code
dies immediately. Codes are good for a few minutes and work only once — if
yours runs out, the computer will offer you a fresh one.

## Only approve a code you can see yourself

This is the one part worth reading twice.

Approving a code hands that browser access to your account — your moments, your
Mains, your credits. So approve only a code that is on a screen **in front of
you**, on a computer that is **yours**.

If someone sends you a code, reads one out to you, or shows you a page and asks
you to scan it, say no. There is no situation where another person needs you to
approve a sign-in code for them. That is true of every service that works this
way, not just this one.

The four-character check exists to make this a deliberate choice rather than a
reflex: you're confirming *this* computer, the one you're sitting at.

## What's different on a computer

Almost everything works the same: the map, posting, appreciating, comments,
connections, crews and vibes.

Two things stay on your phone, because they need your World Money wallet:

- **Topping up energy credits.** Buying credits happens through World Money.
- **The Vault.** Claiming $MAIN happens in World Money and requires Orb
  verification.

You'll simply not see those options in a browser, rather than find them and hit
a wall. Your daily free credits still arrive as normal, and you can spend them
from either place.

## How long a computer stays signed in

A signed-in computer stays signed in for about half a day, then asks again — a
good deal shorter than your phone, because a computer is more likely to be
shared or left open.

You can sign out from the computer at any time. There is currently no way to
sign a computer out remotely from your phone, so if you've used a machine you
don't control, sign out before you walk away.
